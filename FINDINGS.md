# Preliminary Findings (Pilot Sample)

Aggregate results from the manual annotation of 100 marker-oriented records from LANA-CASE. This file contains counts only; no corpus text, transcripts, or record-linked data are included (see the README for the data-sharing policy).

## How to read these results

- The sample is exploratory and marker-oriented: 10 records were randomly selected per target marker. The counts below describe this sample and are **not** estimates of how often the markers occur or are used in LANA-CASE or in spoken American English.
- 93 of the 100 records are in the core analysis (53 confirmed, 40 provisional). The other 7 are excluded from it: 4 are explicit exclusions and 3 are non-epistemic controls. By marker, the 7 are *must* (4), *could* (1), *might* (1) and *I thought* (1), which is why *must* has 6 core records, *I thought*, *might* and *could* have 9, and the other markers have 10.
- Stance-family labels are working organising categories, not a final taxonomy. Each record carries one sample-level label, and token-level validation is still pending (see METHODOLOGY.md).
- The counts are descriptive. No inferential tests or inter-annotator reliability coefficient are reported in this version.
- Primary and secondary function codes are not summarised here. They are still proposed codes and have not been assigned to every record.

## 4. Observations from this sample

1. **Some markers map closely onto one family.** *I thought* (9 of 9), *might* (9 of 9) and *could* (8 of 9) were almost entirely labelled possibility or past belief. *Maybe* and *probably* were mostly labelled possibility (7 of 10) and probability (7 of 10), with the remaining three records of each labelled inference / possible explanation.
2. **The first-person constructions are more varied.** *I think* was most often labelled tentative belief / personal assessment (6 of 10) but also received four other labels. *I guess* was the most dispersed marker, with six different labels across ten records and three records left context-dependent.
3. **In this sample, *I know* was mainly interactional.** Eight of its ten core records were labelled interactional acknowledgment / alignment, and only one was labelled knowledge / lack of knowledge.
4. ***I don't know* split evenly** between knowledge / lack of knowledge (5) and uncertainty / lack of certainty (5). Mitigating uses are recorded in the coding notes and are not distinguished at the stance-family level (see CODEBOOK.md).
5. ***Must* had the highest rate of non-epistemic use.** Four of its ten records were excluded as non-epistemic necessity or intention, which is four of the seven exclusions overall. Among its six core records, four were labelled inference.
6. **The two most frequent response labels were ELA and ACK**, together covering 57 of the 93 core records (61%). DIS (7) appeared mainly after *I guess* (3) and *I thought* (2); AGR (6) mainly after *must* (3) and *maybe* (2). These numbers are small and are reported descriptively only.

## 5. Limitations specific to these results

- Validation is uneven across markers (section 3). Results for *I think*, *I guess* and *I know* rest mostly on provisional labels, whereas *maybe* and *I don't know* are almost entirely confirmed.
- Per-marker counts are small (6 to 10 records), so percentages within a marker should not be over-interpreted.
- One record labelled non-epistemic necessity / intention is retained provisionally in the core set; it is counted under *must* above pending review.
